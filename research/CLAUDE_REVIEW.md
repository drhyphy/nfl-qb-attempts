# Claude Fable challenger review — September 6, 2026

Claude's model is a credible challenger with a stronger historical-odds research foundation than Astra V2. The available evidence does not establish that either will win prospectively. The headline forecasting gap is mostly an evaluation-population difference, and Claude's headline betting ROI does not reproduce its production policy exactly.

Claude minus Astra MAE is −0.0412 attempts; a 5,000-replicate game-block bootstrap across 272 games gives a 95% interval of **−0.1898 to +0.1089**. This is a small, statistically uncertain advantage. The seven extra rows in Astra's original 544-row benchmark are all zero-attempt scheduled starters, which raises its original MAE to 6.9715. The saved forecasts differ in training populations, feature construction, regularization and in-season EB updating, so this comparison measures those complete forecast designs together. 2025 is now exposed historical evidence, not a new untouched test.

## What Claude does well

- **Found a real historical prop archive.** The BettingPros archive enables comparisons to actual quoted prices, assessment of model information beyond the market, and tests of blending weights. Astra V2 has not yet earned its provisional 35%/50% probability weights against historical prop prices. Claude's saved development regression slope is 0.3937, and the archive's model/market MAEs are close; its restrained interpretation of the incremental signal is appropriate. Source: Claude [market backtest output](../challenger/frozen/market_backtest_summary.json), [src/market_backtest.py:138-175](../challenger/source/market_backtest.py#L138).
- **Uses cross-line information.** It inverts each book's de-vigged price and line into a distribution location, then takes a market median. This can recognize a book a full attempt away from consensus even when no peer offers exactly the same line; Astra's exact-line peer requirement misses such opportunities. The tradeoff is greater dependence on the assumed residual distribution. Source: [src/market_backtest.py:112-117](../challenger/source/market_backtest.py#L112), [src/edges.py:133-140](../challenger/source/edges.py#L133).
- **Separates ordinary workload from previous early exits.** QB level averages use games with at least 85% of team attempts, while exit frequency remains a separate predictor and early exits remain in outcome labels. This is a sensible candidate mechanism, rather than simply deleting all early-exit losses. Share of attempts is still an imperfect proxy for playing time. Source: [src/features.py:130-149,216-245](../challenger/source/features.py#L130).
- **Substantial temporal discipline and useful restraint.** One history path enforces a six-hour lag; model and residual distributions train on earlier seasons. Ridge and per-QB empirical-Bayes correction beat the more complicated candidate models in development; a later tendency experiment was rejected when development results worsened. Source: [src/features.py:297-317](../challenger/source/features.py#L297), [src/model.py:174-219,237-269](../challenger/source/model.py#L174), `PLAYBOOK.md` §4b.

## Material critiques

1. **The betting-policy 2025 result is not untouched.** The policy file explicitly says the 0.35 mean-blending weight was chosen with 2025 log-loss results visible. The playbook acknowledges this, but README and some evaluation labels continue to call 2025 untouched. This does not invalidate all forecasting validation; it does mean the final policy's +11.47% archive ROI is an exposed benchmark, not an independent proof of profitability. Source: [original policy](../challenger/frozen/policy.json) field `basis`; [src/policy_analysis.py:29-39,65-74](../challenger/source/policy_analysis.py#L29).

2. **The archive betting test and live probability engine use different distributions.** The market backtest uses prior `ridge` residuals and always fits a heteroscedastic scale model; policy analysis prices using those saved scales. Production selects `ridge_eb`, uses that candidate's residuals, and disables scaling (`use_scale_model: false`). Both the inferred market center and tail probabilities can change. The exact live distribution should be replayed before attaching historical ROI to it. Source: [src/market_backtest.py:97-113,201](../challenger/source/market_backtest.py#L97); [src/policy_analysis.py:11,17-18,44](../challenger/source/policy_analysis.py#L11); [src/model.py:267-269](../challenger/source/model.py#L267); [original validation](../challenger/frozen/validation.json).

3. **The advertised 668 bets are qualifying book quotes, not 668 first-decision production picks.** The policy analysis loops over every real-book row with at least three books and EV bounds. It does not apply production's NY-only, −250 price, 36-hour age or six-attempt disagreement limits, nor the final one-QB/one-game selection. Correlated offers are not inherently invalid to study, and the week bootstrap helps, but this is a different portfolio from the daily card. Source: [src/policy_analysis.py:12,41-52](../challenger/source/policy_analysis.py#L12); compare [src/edges.py:149-178](../challenger/source/edges.py#L149). Saved [policy analysis output](../challenger/frozen/policy_analysis.json) reports development +3.3332% on 776 quotes and 2025 +11.4683% on 668 quotes.

4. **Historical availability remains unproven, especially in 2025.** The loader admits updates up to 15 minutes after scheduled kickoff (`age_h >= −0.25`). In the reviewed `output/market_backtest_quotes.parquet`, 3,513 of 3,536 real-book 2025 rows (99.35%) have negative age. Those may be aggregator closing bookkeeping, rather than live prices; the data alone cannot distinguish this. Restricting age to “within six hours” still retains negative ages. Closing forecasts and asynchronously last-updated book offers do not reconstruct a common 6:30 a.m. decision set. The claim that positive closing ROI is “very likely” positive earlier is therefore stronger than the demonstrated evidence: earlier execution also has different information, lines and availability. Source: [src/market_backtest.py:55-63,179-183](../challenger/source/market_backtest.py#L55); `PLAYBOOK.md` §1. The current saved file contains 8,493 real-book rows, so the README's 8,114 figure is also not the current file count.

5. **Several production checks are weaker than Astra's.** Claude's live merge discards the provider kickoff and matches by player name/team, so it does not independently require the quote's exact event to match the scheduled event. It forms its market center before stale-quote gates; duplicate sources at different lines can give one book multiple median votes. Missing update timestamps pass the age gate, and QB1 depth-chart membership plus a listed prop is the only starter check—there is no explicit active/injury gate or depth-chart freshness check. Astra's canonical event, independent starter, current game-market and freshness controls are stronger. Preserve Claude's numerical model while sharing those operational checks in the challenger runner. Source: [src/edges.py:72-79,125-155](../challenger/source/edges.py#L72); `src/odds.py:217-219`. New-team and coach-change flags are informational only, which is a policy choice, not inherently an error.

6. **Production updating has concrete implementation defects.** `build_dataset()` defaults to 2017–2025 and the script calls that default, so the stated weekly rebuild does not include completed 2026 games. If that is corrected, `fit_only()` has a further issue: it repeatedly reads the unchanged historical OOF file, recomputes every new-season row using the previous artifact (which may already have trained on earlier rows), and does not persist the OOF extension. Those residuals can become partly in-sample. Additionally, historical EB updates use unadjusted ridge residuals, while production EB receives the already EB-adjusted residual pool. These are train/serve discrepancies to fix in a separately versioned adapter or avoid with an explicitly frozen launch artifact. Source: [src/features.py:321-334](../challenger/source/features.py#L321); [src/model.py:198-204,267-269,291-315](../challenger/source/model.py#L198).

## Additional limitations to retain

- Claude's zero-attempt/no-carry exclusion is not direct proof of zero snaps; a sacks-only appearance is a possible counterexample. Its ledger currently assigns zero attempts to a missing player row and settles the bet, rather than mirroring its stated scratch-void treatment. Source: [src/features.py:131-134](../challenger/source/features.py#L131); [src/ledger.py:65-75](../challenger/source/ledger.py#L65). Astra's zero-starter target has the opposite potential mismatch; neither side should count a proven nonparticipant as a win/loss.
- Lagging nflfastR `xpass`-derived features prevents direct target-game use, but the upstream model's fitting vintage is not documented here. This is an unresolved as-of provenance question, not demonstrated leakage. Astra excludes those derived values until provenance is established. Source: [src/features.py:93-97,253-254,266](../challenger/source/features.py#L93).
- The live runner claims an ESPN spread/total fallback in its docstring, but the inspected `build_requests()` simply skips missing schedule numbers. Its depth-chart filename is also hard-coded to 2026. Source: [src/edges.py:5-6,73,85-87](../challenger/source/edges.py#L5).
- Claude's cross-line CLV is a model-imputed metric using the latest artifact's residual shape, rather than a direct same-line closing price. Calling it CLV should disclose that distinction. Source: [src/clv.py:52,59-72](../challenger/source/clv.py#L52).

## Challenger interpretation

Keep Claude's launch ridge/EB coefficients, residual pool and 0.35 mean-blending/dead-zone policy identifiable; do not silently retune it based on today's card. Score both designs on the same fresh canonical offers and shared availability checks, archive each model's first decisions separately, and publish paired forecast accuracy/calibration and independently graded betting returns. Show native policy holds separately from shared source-quality holds. Claude deserves a genuine prospective opportunity; its historical archive work is a useful next research direction for Astra too.

## Daily integration and prospective comparison

The daily site runs `claude-qbatt-v1.0-adapter1` as a challenger, preserving the numerical launch ridge estimator, saved EB corrections, residual distribution, cross-line center, 0.35 mean blend, 1.5-attempt dead zone and native qualification limits. The portable representation is checked against Claude's saved forecast. Source hashes, the original Git revision and the adapter's differences are preserved under `challenger/`.

Both models receive the same canonically resolved public offers, retrieval/decision time and fresh ESPN game markets. The adapter adds common starting-role, active/injury and source freshness checks, and exposes those separately from native policy reasons. Its card is therefore Claude's policy on shared eligible inputs, not a claim of byte-for-byte equivalence to the original standalone runner's data coverage. Both cards currently use the same ScoresAndOdds quotes; Claude's additional BettingPros live feed is not mixed into only one side of the contest.

The update path uses the current season explicitly, refreshes causal records and refits only the chosen base estimator. Launch residuals and saved EB corrections stay frozen; it does not repeatedly manufacture in-sample residuals through the original `fit_only` path. This is a documented operational adaptation. It does not select a new candidate, tune the blend, or claim the original archive ROI applies to the adapted card.

Recommendation returns use separate immutable first decisions per QB/game, shared exact-line near-kickoff prices, flat one-unit stakes and missing-stat abstention. The champion's prior solo history is preserved but excluded from the head-to-head ROI period. A model failure is visible and never substitutes a previous card as today's recommendation.

For paired forecast scoring, use the earliest valid shared pregame snapshot per QB/game and choose a common offered book/line lexicographically, independently of EV or qualification. This includes held offers. Point MAE uses both independent forecast means; Brier/log loss use each final conditional over probability at the same line. Integer-line pushes remain in outcome records and are excluded from these conditional binary scores. The deterministic reference need not be either model's recommended offer. No winner or confidence interval is inferred from an immature prospective sample.
